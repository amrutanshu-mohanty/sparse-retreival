Part 3: Seeing Vocabulary Mismatch
•​ Pull cases where BM25 fails to retrieve the gold document in the top-k. Characterize why
they fail (examples could be synonymy, paraphrasing, abbreviation vs. expansion, etc.).
•​ Compute lexical overlap (Jaccard coefficient over query and gold-document tokens) and
compare the distribution for query–document pairs where BM25 succeeds vs. fails. Report
whether low overlap predicts failure, and how strongly.
•​ Nice to read (but not necessary): this paper by CMU LTI on the original description of the
problem.
Report deliverable: per dataset, a breakdown of failure cases of different types and 2-3
concrete query/gold-document examples for each type you find; a plot or summary statistics
comparing the Jaccard-overlap distribution for BM25-success vs. BM25-failure pairs; and a
short written verdict on whether, and how strongly, low overlap predicts failure.

Part 4: Pseudo-Relevance Feedback — Corpus-Retrieved and
LLM-Generated
Build 4a first, then reuse it unchanged in 4b.

4a. Rocchio & RM3 (corpus-retrieved feedback)
Implement Rocchio's algorithm yourself, using Part 1's IndexReader to pull term-frequency
vectors for feedback documents:
w_t = alpha · f(q)[t] + (beta / N) · Σ_{d ∈ feedback docs} f ̃(d)[t]
•​ Run Part 2's BM25 retrieval, take the top-N results as pseudo-relevant, and filter out overly
common corpus terms (e.g. terms appearing in more than 10% of documents) before
ranking candidate expansion terms.
•​ Construct the re-weighted query as a boosted query via Pyserini's Query Builder API
(rather than string concatenation) and re-search.
•​ You may also use Pyserini's built-in set_rm3() as a second feedback model and as a sanity
check on your own Rocchio numbers.
•​ Report results across at least two settings of N (feedback documents) and k (expansion
terms kept), and discuss query drift — cases where feedback pulls the query toward an
unrelated term.
Report deliverable: a metrics table (nDCG@10, Recall@100, MRR@10, MAP) for Rocchio
and for RM3 across your ≥ 2 (N, k) settings, per dataset, compared against the Part 2 BM25
baseline. Include at least two concrete query-drift examples — the added term(s) and why
they hurt that query.

4b. HyDE (LLM-generated feedback)
HyDE (Gao et al., 2023) prompts an LLM to generate a hypothetical answer document for the
query, then treats that document's terms as expansion terms: the LLM's generated text stands in
for the corpus-retrieved feedback documents from 4a.
•​ Use a small open-weight LLM you can run locally (e.g. a 7B-class (Qwen) model via vLLM,
no need to use a paid API) to sample a few hypothetical documents per query.
•​ Reuse your 4a Rocchio/RM3 implementation unchanged — only the feedback source
(LLM instead of corpus retrieval) changes.
•​ Compare three variants: naive concatenation of query + hypothetical documents, your
Rocchio/RM3-weighted version, and 4a's corpus-based PRF. Report which matters more:
the feedback source (LLM-generated vs. corpus-retrieved) or the combination method
(concatenation vs. feedback model).
Report deliverable: a metrics table (same four metrics) for the three variants — naive
concatenation, Rocchio/RM3-weighted HyDE, and 4a's corpus PRF — per dataset, plus a
short written answer, grounded in the table rather than asserted, to whether feedback source
or combination method matters more.

Part 5: Sparse Retrieval with a Pretrained SPLADE Checkpoint
Take a look at the SPLADE paper and familiarize yourself. Load a pretrained checkpoint from
HuggingFace — naver/splade-v3, or an earlier model such as
naver/splade-cocondenser-ensembledistil — and use it for inference.
For a BERT-like encoder with an MLM head, the sparse representation assigns each vocabulary
term j a weight:
w_j = max_i log(1 + ReLU(o_ij))
where o_ij is the MLM output logit for vocabulary term j at position i. Pyserini's
SpladeQueryEncoder computes this on the query side; check whether a prebuilt SPLADE
impact index already exists for your dataset (for Pyserini) before building your own with --impact
--pretokenized.
•​ Encode and index all three required corpora (SciFact, FEVER, HotpotQA) with your
chosen checkpoint.
•​ Run retrieval and report the same metrics as every other part.
•​ Compare directly against Part 4: for at least 10 queries per dataset, list the top expansion
terms SPLADE assigns non-zero weight to beyond the literal query terms, alongside the
Rocchio/RM3 terms from 4a and the HyDE terms from 4b for the same queries. Report the
term overlap across the three sources and discuss 2-3 cases where they disagree.
Report deliverable: a metrics table for SPLADE per dataset, plus the ≥ 10-query, three-way
expansion-term comparison table (SPLADE vs. Rocchio/RM3 vs. HyDE) with the overlap
stated and the disagreement cases discussed.

Extra Credit (3 points total)
1. Document-Side Expansion (1 point). Everything so far expands the query at retrieval time.
An alternative is to expand the document at indexing time: predict pseudo-queries a document
is likely to be retrieved by, and append them before indexing (Nogueira et al.'s doc2query /
docTTTTTquery). Prompt the same LLM from Part 4b to generate a handful of plausible queries
per document, append them to the document text, rebuild the index, and re-run Part 2's BM25
baseline over it. Report whether this recovers Part 3 failure cases that query-side expansion
(Part 4) didn't, and at what cost — index size and build time — versus query-side expansion's
recurring per-query cost.
Report deliverable: BM25 metrics before vs. after doc2query expansion, the index-size,
and a count of how many Part 3 failure cases the expanded index is able to recover, if at all.
2. Full-Scale MSMARCO (1 point). Repeat Parts 1, 2, and 5 (index, BM25 baseline, SPLADE)
on the full MSMARCO passage corpus. Pyserini ships a prebuilt SPLADE++ impact index for
MSMARCO, so Part 5 here is largely running the existing pipeline at scale rather than rebuilding
it. Report timing and index size alongside retrieval metrics; this item is scored on whether your
Part 1/2/5 pipeline runs unmodified at MSMARCO's scale, not on beating the
SciFact/FEVER/HotpotQA numbers.
Report deliverable: index build time, retrieval latency (per query), index size, and the Part
2/5 metrics table, all on MSMARCO.
3. Training SPLADE (1 point). Instead of the pretrained checkpoint from Part 5, fine-tune your
own SPLADE model: a contrastive ranking loss with in-batch negatives, plus a FLOPS sparsity
regularizer applied separately to the average query and document vectors, with the
regularization weight scheduled (e.g. quadratic warm-up) rather than fixed from the start.
Compare your trained model's expansions against the pretrained checkpoints from Part 5 —
where do they agree, and where does training on your specific corpora change what gets
expanded to?
Report deliverable: a metrics table for your trained model vs. the Part 5 pretrained
checkpoint, per dataset, plus a short comparison of where the expansion terms agree and
where training on your corpora changed them.
